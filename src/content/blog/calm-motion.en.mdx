Motion has a volume knob, and almost every site turns it too high. The bounce, the spring, the stagger that runs for two seconds: each one is a sentence spoken louder than the content it introduces. Calm motion is not the absence of animation. It is animation that knows when to stop.

## One easing, one duration

A site needs two or three durations and two easings, not thirty. When every transition shares the same curve, the interface develops a voice — the same way a typeface does. The curve I keep returning to is slow at both ends: it starts without a jolt and lands without a bounce.

## Reveal on arrival, never on scroll-back

Content should arrive once, then stay. Re-animating every time a section re-enters the viewport makes the page feel nervous, and it punishes the reader for scrolling up to re-read a paragraph. One intersection, one reveal, done.

## Motion is a budget

Every animated element costs attention at the exact moment the visitor is trying to read. Spend it where the hierarchy needs help: a page's first statement, a section that must be noticed, a hover that confirms where the link goes. Everything else stays still.

The test is simple. Scroll the page with the sound off, so to speak, and ask whether any movement competed with the sentence you were reading. If one did, it was too loud.
